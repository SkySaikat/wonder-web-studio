# Wonderweb Studio — launch, pricing and first 30 days

Updated: 7 November 2026. This document supersedes the unconfirmed commercial assumptions in the original plan. It is an operating plan, not a revenue forecast. Website implementation is in `website/`.

## 1. The commercial target

The owner wants **more than $5,000 monthly profit**, with **$4,000–$6,000 monthly operating costs**, and ideally three to four new clients in the first 30 days. Target US home-service businesses first, while accepting small businesses worldwide. English is the initial website language.

Use **$12,000 in collected monthly recurring service revenue** as the planning target. At the higher stated cost level, $11,000 produces exactly $5,000, which does not meet “more than $5,000.” Costs must include the delivery of the newly sold work for this calculation to hold.

This is operating contribution after the owner's stated costs, not a verified after-tax accounting profit. Confirm whether payment fees, taxes, owner compensation, acquisition spend and incremental contractors are already included; subtract anything omitted. Client advertising spend is not agency service revenue. One-time website projects can help cash flow but are not recurring revenue. Signing three retainers near day 30 does not create a full month of revenue earned during the first month.

The comparator floor is not automatically profitable. Before offering a discount, check the total client mix, delivery hours and monthly cost budget. At $6,000 costs, three clients must average **more than $3,666.67 each**. Three Full Studio clients at $3,750 produce $11,250 revenue and $5,250 operating contribution, leaving little room for cost growth. Prefer a narrow scope or phased project over an uncosted discount.

Public pricing starts the conversation. The sales call leads to a written scope, accepted proposal and then a Stripe or PayPal invoice/payment link. No off-the-shelf checkout or automatic subscription enrollment is required. Client media spend is separate and paid directly to the advertising platform.

## 3. Offers to lead with

**Full Studio, from $3,999/month:** website care, local search, Google and Meta management, four content deliverables, eight social posts across two profiles, creative testing and twice-monthly reviews. Starting allowance: one market, two ad accounts, up to $10,000 combined monthly media spend managed. New website builds, filming and community management are separate unless included in the proposal.

**Growth, from $1,999/month:** Foundation scope plus one paid channel, two content deliverables, one landing-page improvement and a monthly strategy review. Starting allowance: one market, one ad account, up to $5,000 monthly media spend managed.

**Website project, from $4,500:** up to five core pages, responsive design, inquiry form and foundational metadata. Content writing, migration and integrations are defined separately. Use project work to build relationships and identify a suitable ongoing care or marketing scope; do not assume every project will convert to a retainer.

Full Studio should be recommended only where the business has sufficient advertising budget, capacity and usable assets. A business that only needs a website should receive a website proposal.

### Content quality rules

Each page needs a distinct purpose, specific useful information and a relevant next step. Do not create a separate URL for every keyword variation. Keep original search exports and the full [SEO inventory](WONDERWEB_STUDIO_SEO_INVENTORY.md) as the backlog; the 71-page launch does not exhaust every relevant cluster.

More words are not a ranking mechanism by themselves. Follow [Google's people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content): answer real questions, explain the work accurately and avoid padding. FAQs help visitors; do not promise FAQ rich results for an ordinary agency website. Avoid unsupported city pages, fake offices, fabricated reviews, unverified awards and “best agencies” rankings written only to place Wonderweb first.

Launch guides include costs and comparisons, a redesign checklist, management versus maintenance, a content calendar, nine home-service marketing ideas, 11 website trust signals, seven inquiry improvements, eight post ideas, nine agency-selection questions and seven thumbnail checks.

## 5. Thirty-day acquisition plan

Count day 1 from the day the production site and inquiry route are functional. If that is 7 November, the window is 7 November–6 December 2026. The goal is three to four **paid, deliverable engagements**, with sufficient recurring revenue—not merely three low-value accounts.

SEO is a continuing acquisition investment. The first-month plan also uses the existing audience, genuine past relationships, referrals and individually researched prospects. This document drafts activity; no messages, ads or posts have been sent automatically.

| Period | Owner/sales work | Website/content work | Evidence to review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–3 | Identify 30 genuine warm contacts; choose two home-service segments; cost the offers | Activate delivery/tracking, check mobile forms, prepare one-page audit outline | Real inbox receipt; accurate offers; delivery capacity |
| Days 4–7 | Personally contact 15 suitable warm relationships; request 5 introductions; offer specific audits | Publish a relaunch announcement, first project breakdown and a useful checklist | Replies, qualified conversations, booked calls |
| Days 8–14 | Research 25 suitable businesses; produce 5 short personalized audits; conduct calls | Share one home-service project, one campaign story with context, one FAQ post and one short video | Held calls, proposals, objections, best-performing content |
| Days 15–21 | Follow up on relevant replies; deliver proposals within an agreed turnaround; seek additional referrals | Publish a buyer guide, behind-the-scenes post, offer explanation and a second short video | Proposal acceptance, expected delivery cost, first payments |
| Days 22–30 | Close suitable proposals; onboard paid clients; review capacity before further discounting | Publish a second case breakdown, practical checklist and onboarding/process post | Collected recurring revenue, actual costs, inquiry quality |

Daily operating rhythm for the founder/sales owner: review new inquiries; respond with a specific next step; research or follow up with a small number of suitable businesses; record stages and next actions. Reserve delivery time before promising a start date. Content/design support prepares assets in batches with owner approval.

### Pipeline scenarios, not industry benchmarks

For planning, 20 held qualified calls at a 20% close rate would produce four clients. At a 70% attendance rate that needs about 29 booked calls. These rates are assumptions to test, not claims about expected performance. An alternative warm pipeline might close four from 12 held calls, but cannot be assumed. Track actual conversion rates weekly and revise activity.

A weekly scorecard should include: new qualified inquiries, booked calls, held calls, proposals, accepted proposals, collected revenue, contracted recurring revenue, delivery commitments, lead source and estimated contribution. Record why unsuitable leads were declined. A $12,000 signed pipeline with no payment is not $12,000 collected revenue.

### Qualification and sales conversation

1. Understand the business, priority service, real service area, capacity and target customer.
2. Review the current website, traffic sources, inquiry process and usable assets.
3. Clarify the budget split between agency service fees and advertising spend.
4. Identify one first priority and the evidence needed to judge progress.
5. Recommend the smallest coherent scope that solves the problem; show expansion options only where useful.
6. Check delivery cost and the discount floor before presenting an offer.
7. Send the proposal with scope, exclusions, timeline, responsibilities, payment terms and review points.
8. After acceptance, issue the agreed invoice and onboard access. Keep the business's accounts and assets clearly documented.
