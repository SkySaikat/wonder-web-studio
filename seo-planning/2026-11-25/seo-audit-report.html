Your next SEO move

  • Improve existing appliance-repair marketing and landscaping PPC pages before expanding the city-page program.
  • Preserve the useful custom-home-builder SEO guide and give its readers a clearer path to the service.
  • Review the 15 new local pages, then publish and measure the pilot when the owner is ready.

Google Search Console reports 9,726 U.S. impressions, zero clicks and average position 57.6 for October 26–November 22, 2026. Google is showing pages, but most visibility is too low to earn clicks; more page count alone is not the solution.

Recommendations

1. Strengthen two existing service pages with real buying-intent visibility

Do this

  • Improve appliance-repair marketing: remove repeated passages and explain service scope, repair intake and lead qualification.
  • Improve landscaping PPC: separate maintenance, installations, territories, ad spend and management responsibilities.
  • Use verified project evidence and the actual written offer; keep pricing and a project conversation easy to reach.

Why

  • These searches explicitly seek agency help, so their business fit is stronger than generic software research.
  • Query-to-page data confirms the existing destinations; Google inspection confirms both are indexed.
  • Clearer scope and relevant proof could improve relevance and inquiry quality. The small samples do not support a traffic or revenue forecast.

3. Test a bounded Texas and existing-market website-design pilot

Do this

  • Review nine distinct web-design pages: Texas, Dallas, Plano, Fort Worth, Houston, Austin, Phoenix, Tampa and Orlando.
  • Review three location hubs and three original buyer articles before the owner publishes.
  • Keep one primary page per intent, link pricing and relevant services, and expand only when evidence supports it.

Why

  • These pages explain business decisions and service scope, giving prospective buyers a usable landing page.
  • Texas is the owner's priority; existing Florida and Arizona hubs provide a coherent first test.
  • Dedicated competing pages exist. Search volume and keyword difficulty remain unavailable, so these are experiments rather than verified easy markets.

Evidence and methodology

Search Console: verified sc-domain:wonderwebstudio.com, web search, final data, United States. Windows: August 22–November 22 and October 26–November 22, 2026, Pacific dates. The country aggregates match at 9,726 impressions and zero clicks; this is not evidence of growth between periods.

The longer U.S. export returned 1,715 distinct query rows and 420 page rows after pagination. Query privacy filtering and reporting limits mean these are not all searches. Country aggregates, page rows and query rows were not added together.

Inspect URLs: homepage, Dallas hub, appliance-repair marketing, landscaping PPC and custom-home-builder guide passed stored index inspection on November 25. Google-selected and declared preferred URLs matched. The new Dallas web-design destination was unknown. This is stored Google status, not a live crawl or a full-site inspection.

Crawl: OpenSEO audit 470c424a-533b-448d-a0bf-e600e5b689dc completed 50 pages, non-rendered, no Lighthouse. No critical issue in the sample; 852 discovered URLs remained uncrawled.

Keyword research: five submitted seeds and the domain overview failed for insufficient credits. Volume, cost per click, difficulty and provider traffic estimates are unknown. No paid tracking or credit purchase was enabled.

Public competition: November 25 searches checked buyer-oriented queries across Dallas, Plano, Fort Worth, Frisco, McKinney, Denton, Round Rock, Katy, Pearland, Lakeland, Chandler, Greenville and Cary, plus Dallas roofing and Texas HVAC combinations. These were competitor-presence checks, not localized Google position measurements.

Examples: Dallas web design, Plano, Fort Worth contractor positioning, Dallas roofing design, Round Rock.

Content review: appliance-repair marketing repeats passages; landscaping PPC has broad outcome claims; the builder guide answers several specific buyer questions. These observations informed the next edits but do not prove a cause of low rankings. Historical cross-brand URLs in Search Console do not prove present content: the old local-SEO slug already redirects in local configuration.

SEO rules: relevant imagery helps explain content; a high image count is not a target. Images can be nested in a normal page sitemap. Structured data describes visible facts; Markdown and AI files do not guarantee indexing, ranking or AI citations.

Primary guidance: Google AI features, Google Images, image sitemaps, doorway and scaled-content policies.
