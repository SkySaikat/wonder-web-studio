## Interpretation and editorial boundaries

The first ten city guides are supported by dedicated source location pages. Indianapolis and Miami come from location-bearing source case-study URLs. Florida appears in search queries; Indiana appears in a case-study URL. The latter signals are weaker than direct location-page demand and do not establish current client work or an office in those markets. Los Angeles also has med-spa web-design queries; the new general location guide does not claim medical-sector expertise or promise to satisfy that specialist intent.

The source list informs priorities, not a ranking forecast. Each new page has a distinct local brief and clearly describes remote delivery. Nearby place names are coverage-planning examples, not claimed offices or verified client locations. No country pages are generated from audience country reports alone.

Content direction follows [Google’s people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) and [doorway-abuse guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#doorway-abuse): distinct useful destinations, clear hierarchy and no automatic city-name substitutions.
